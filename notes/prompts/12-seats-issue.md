# This file provides an introduction on why does the JAAC (Joint Awami Action Committe) wants to demolish the 12 refugee seats.

Video Link: https://youtu.be/4IRR5kTAbKE?si=OK7l-p3jKlB0nBT-

The seats are a reward for insiders, not real representation

These 12 seats were never really about representing refugees — they became a way to reward a small "elite" group with power and access to Kashmir's resources. What started as a kind of political payoff has spiraled: now every single member elected to these seats expects to become a minister, so instead of a lean government fighting for Kashmir's freedom, you end up with a bloated cabinet of 60-70 ministers, all justified in the name of "the struggle."

Many of the people holding these seats may not even qualify

According to Sardar Zahid, of the 12 people currently holding these seats, only 4 are genuinely verified as people with real refugee/"state subject" status. Another 4 have doubtful paperwork, and 4 are outright not state subjects at all. And underneath all of this is a bigger, unresolved question: AJK operates under an "Interim Constitution" from 1974 that AJK's own people never got to vote on or shape — because the larger question of Jammu & Kashmir's future was never actually settled.

Pakistan already handles the "refugee" issues — through a separate agreement

Under the 1949 Karachi Agreement, subjects like foreign policy, defense, and resettling refugees who moved to Pakistan were handed over to the Government of Pakistan itself — not the AJK Assembly. So legally, refugee affairs were never supposed to be AJK's responsibility in the first place. The 12 seats were created later, through a 1970 election law, and then locked into the constitution through the 13th Amendment — specifically, the argument goes, to block a legal challenge to them that was raised in 2015.

The whole system runs on a fiction

The actual refugees from 1947 are no longer alive. Their descendants — who should be the ones benefiting, if anyone — are often pushed aside by people who use the "refugee" label purely for political advantage, without having any real claim to it.

No representation without taxation

There's a basic fairness principle being violated here: these 12 representatives don't even live in the territory (stretching from Bhimber to Taobut) that they help govern and tax. Meanwhile, the actual residents of that territory have no equivalent voice in whatever process elects those 12 people.

A "triple advantage" for a few, at everyone else's expense

Some people end up with three separate sets of opportunities at once: as a resident of their home province in Pakistan, as a Pakistani citizen generally, and as an AJK representative through these seats. Meanwhile, an ordinary Kashmiri student or worker — who only has access to opportunities within AJK — is stuck with far less. That's the opposite of equal treatment under the law.

These seats don't even protect the Kashmir cause — they undermine it

UN Security Council resolutions are clear that whatever temporary administrative setups exist in Kashmir now don't affect the final plebiscite (the vote that's supposed to eventually decide Kashmir's future). So the seats can't be defended as "protecting" the Kashmir cause. And since defense and foreign policy are already Pakistan's job, not AJK's, there's no real justification left for these 12 seats to exist at all — they just create room for favoritism and exploitation.
